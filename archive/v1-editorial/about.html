About RoleColorFinder

Every team has a shape. We help leaders see it.

RoleColorFinder is a team-dynamics platform built on a patent-pending leadership assessment. It shows how each person on a team prefers to work, which pairs are likely to grind against each other, and what kind of person the team is missing.

It rests on an idea from group psychology: a team needs different things from its leaders at different points in its life. A personality label can’t show you that. A picture of the whole team can.

What we believe

Four ideas shape the assessment, the reports, and every tool we’ve built on top of them.

  1. 01

    Leadership depends on context.

    A style that steadies a new team can smother an experienced one. Good leaders adjust to what the team needs right now, so a useful assessment has to show more than a fixed type.

  2. 02

    The pair is where work happens.

    Two people can each be excellent and still grind against each other every week. Those patterns are predictable, so we name them and pair each one with a fix.

  3. 03

    Color describes how, not how well.

    A RoleColor profile describes how someone works. It is never a score. We look at the shape of a team, never at the worth of the people on it.

  4. 04

    A label is only the start.

    Knowing your color is step one. Knowing what it does to the people around you is the point, which is why our reports focus on what to do next.

Where pairs collide

Four profiles make ten possible pairings. Each one has a name, a typical way of breaking down, and a fix. Three are rated high-friction.

  • Direction trap

    Executor × Visionary

    Blue keeps improving the idea while Yellow is already trying to ship it. Each sees the other as either premature closure or endless drift.

  • Speed trap

    Motivator × Architect

    Red experiences rigor as delay. Green experiences speed as unforced error. Both believe they are protecting the team from the other.

  • Authority clash

    Motivator × Visionary

    Blue wants room to reshape the direction. Red wants to move people into alignment quickly. When ownership is fuzzy, meetings turn into a contest.

Grounded in how teams develop

The diagnostic is designed around Bruce Tuckman’s model of team development, which has shaped how groups are studied and managed for six decades. Tuckman described four stages in 1965, and he and Mary Ann Jensen added a fifth in 1977.1,2

We combine it with research on contextual and adaptive leadership, which starts from the same premise: what a team needs from its leaders changes as the team changes.

  1. 01

    Forming

    Building connection

  2. 02

    Storming

    Navigating friction

  3. 03

    Norming

    Establishing flow

  4. 04

    Performing

    Doing the best work

  5. 05

    Adjourning

    Ending with clarity

  1. 1.Tuckman, B. W. (1965). Developmental sequence in small groups. Psychological Bulletin, 63(6), 384–399.
  2. 2.Tuckman, B. W., & Jensen, M. A. C. (1977). Stages of small-group development revisited. Group & Organization Studies, 2(4), 419–427.
