01The idea

Every personality test stops at the person. We keep going.

Most teams don’t fail from a lack of talent. They fail from how they work together. Knowing your color is step one. Knowing what it does to the people around you is the point.

  1. Level 1

    The person

    Your RoleColor is your default way of leading, deciding and working with people. Your report ranks all four colors, not just the winner. Useful, and also where most tools stop.

  2. Level 2

    The pair

    Where friction actually lives. Two good people with different thresholds for “ready” can stall a project without either one doing anything wrong.

  3. Level 3

    The team

    Every working relationship, mapped and ranked by risk, with the fix for each one and the profile that would close the gap.

Direction trap

High friction

Blue keeps expanding the horizon while Yellow wants a stable target and a sequence to execute.

Blue often improves the idea while Yellow is already trying to ship it. Each person experiences the other as either premature closure or endless drift.

Good at together

  • This pair can turn bold ideas into real execution when handoffs are explicit.
  • Blue helps Yellow avoid narrow thinking; Yellow helps Blue avoid vapor.
  • They are strong in zero-to-one work that still needs disciplined delivery.

Where it breaks

  • Priorities can keep changing once Yellow has already committed resources.
  • Blue may feel constrained too early, while Yellow feels rework piling up.
  • Trust drops when promise dates move without a shared reset.

What to do

  1. Separate idea generation from execution commitment in the workflow.
  2. Agree on the version that is shipping now versus ideas parked for later.
  3. Use written change logs when scope shifts.

05The four profiles

Four profiles. None of them is the good one.

Color describes how people work, not how well. Each profile is the reason someone is exceptional at part of the job and genuinely hard work in another. Reports rank all four, not just the winner.

EYellow

The Executor

Closes the gap between deciding and doing. The person a stalled project gets handed to.

Optimizes for
Clear priorities, ownership and delivery
Blind spot
Speed can outrun context
Brings
Follow-through, sequencing, accountability
Common among
Founders, builders, PMs

Cadence: clear handoffs, rapid execution loops

MRed

The Motivator

Moves people first and plans second. Makes a team believe the work is worth doing.

Optimizes for
People momentum, alignment and visible energy
Blind spot
Optimism can outrun structure
Brings
Social energy, urgency, buy-in
Common among
Speakers, creatives, brand builders

Cadence: live discussion, fast relational feedback

AGreen

The Architect

Builds the structure everyone else quietly relies on. Wants order that survives scrutiny.

Optimizes for
Logic, quality and durable systems
Blind spot
Analysis can delay commitment
Brings
Rigor, precision, thoughtful tradeoffs
Common among
Analysts, engineers, operators

Cadence: time to think, validate and refine

VBlue

The Visionary

Questions the problem before solving it. Reliably asks the thing nobody else asked.

Optimizes for
Possibility, pattern recognition and innovation
Blind spot
Ideas can drift without a landing zone
Brings
New angles, reframing, future-state thinking
Common among
Strategists, designers, researchers

Cadence: wide exploration before narrowing
